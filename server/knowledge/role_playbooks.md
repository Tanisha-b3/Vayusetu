# Role playbooks: what each organisation can realistically do

## Ward Officer
<!-- role: ward_officer -->
Municipal / ward-level authority with enforcement and service powers. Typical levers: inspect construction sites for dust-control compliance (covering, water sprinkling, barricades); coordinate mechanical sweeping and water sprinkling on dusty roads; act on open waste-burning complaints and deploy a rapid response team; check traffic congestion points and diversions near hotspots; deploy or request additional monitoring (mobile or low-cost sensors) at hotspots and at locations with missing or unreliable readings; coordinate with health department and schools; issue public advisories; escalate repeated hotspots to the city or state pollution control board. Always pair an action with the evidence that justifies it and a verification step.

## Housing Society (RWA)
<!-- role: housing_society -->
Resident welfare association with control over its own premises. Typical levers: avoid sweeping dust dry, use wet sweeping; stop burning leaves and garden waste inside the society; ask contractors to cover materials and sprinkle water during repairs; keep generators serviced and limit their use; maintain trees and green cover; notice-board and group message alerts to residents; set up a clean indoor space for older adults and children; reduce idling at gates; report visible waste burning or construction dust nearby to the ward office with photos and times.

## School
<!-- role: school -->
Schools protect children in their care. Typical levers: shift sports, drill and assemblies indoors on Poor or worse days; cancel or shorten outdoor activities; allow students with asthma to stay indoors and keep inhalers accessible; inform parents through the school app or SMS about the air quality status; keep windows closed during peak pollution hours and ventilate when air is better; ask staff to avoid burning waste on campus; arrange for a trained staff member to watch for symptoms; follow health department and education department guidance on closures.

## How actions should be written
Each action must have an owner, a timeframe (today, this week, this month), a reason tied to evidence, and must stay within the powers of the selected role. Do not give a housing society or a school tasks that only a municipal authority can perform; instead suggest they report or request it.
