# Writing for different languages and reading levels

## Simple level
Aim for roughly grade 5 reading. Short sentences (under 12 words). Everyday words. Avoid abbreviations: say "tiny dust particles" instead of PM2.5, and name the band in plain words such as "air is very bad today". Give 3 to 5 clear actions. Suitable for children, older adults and people with limited literacy.

## Standard level
Clear, general-public language. Name the air quality band, the affected areas and the main precautions. Briefly explain PM2.5 as fine dust particles when first used.

## Detailed level
Technical readers. Include pollutant names, values with units (ug/m3), comparison with the previous report, data-quality notes and limitations.

## Language rules
Write the whole document in the requested language and script. Keep place names recognisable (use the name as in the data; add a transliteration in brackets if helpful). Keep numbers as Western digits (0-9) so values match the data. Keep unit symbols (ug/m3) and the abbreviations PM2.5 / AQI, with a short local-language explanation. Use respectful, calm tone; avoid panic. For Hindi use Devanagari and common words (for example "वायु गुणवत्ता" for air quality, "सावधानी" for precaution). Do not machine-translate word for word; write naturally.

## Care for vulnerable groups
Always include separate short guidance for children, older adults and people sensitive to poor air, even on better days (with lighter advice). Never use fear or blame. Say when to seek medical help.
